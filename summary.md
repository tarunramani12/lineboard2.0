## 1. What this is

LineBoard 2.0 is a web app that takes a film from script to call sheet and shares the plan with the crew: script, scenes, shot list, storyboard, breakdown, cast and crew, props and equipment, schedule, call sheet. It is a rebuild of LineBoard v1 (a single HTML file on an iPad, no accounts). The owner is Tarun Ramani, a filmmaker. He uses it on an iPad (landscape and portrait), a laptop and sometimes a phone.

Source of truth for requirements: `docs/planning-document.md` (part 1 is the brief, part 2 is the design system). Base design: `docs/base-design.md`. Logo rules: `docs/LOGO.md` and `docs/lineboard-brand/` (logo, icons, favicons). Older prototype notes are in memory only.

## 3. Product decisions

**People and permissions** (enforced in the database with row level security and triggers, never only in the screen)
- Roles: owner, admin, member. Member and Viewer were merged. A member joins by invite email or join code and is read-only by default.
- 10 default teams per project (Direction is flagged as the AD team; Camera, Production, Lighting and grip, Sound, Art, Costume, Makeup and hair, VFX, Post). Teams can be added.
- Each team has a level (none, view, edit) per section. Seven sections are controllable: Script, Shot list and storyboard, Cast and crew, Props and equipment, Breakdown, Schedule, Call sheets. Overview and Scenes are visible to everyone on the project. Team and access is owner and admin only. Export follows what each person may see. Defaults: view on Schedule, Call sheets, Cast and crew; none elsewhere.
- Editing needs the team's `edit` level plus being head of department (HOD) or holding a per-person edit grant from the HOD (`member_teams.can_edit`). HOD is a per-team flag. There is no fixed "first assistant" role.
- Script text is shared scene by scene: owner and admins read everything; others read the scenes the owner shared (`scene_access`, `scene_access_scenes`). Everyone on the project sees every heading.
- Crew without accounts (day players) can be cast and crew rows with name and email and receive the call sheet as an emailed PDF. No public links. Later they can link to an account by verified email.
- Account deletion is blocked while the user owns a live project. Deleting the account asks for the password again. Changing the password also asks for the current password.
- Sign out lives only in Account settings (he asked for it there, not in the top bar).

**Hosting**: free-tier Supabase in the EU. Risks to handle later: the project pauses after about 7 days of inactivity (needs a keep-alive), no backups (needs a backup export), storage and egress limits (image cap, not set), built-in email only a few messages an hour (use a free SMTP service such as Resend on a subdomain of his domain). Pricing is parked.

**Script model**
- A script is stored as scenes plus typed elements (action, character, dialogue, parenthetical, transition). Scene text lives in `scene_text` (one row per scene, JSON elements). Number, heading and status live on `scenes`. A scene id is permanent.
- Writing is Fountain in a text box beside a live screenplay preview. One person edits a scene at a time (a per-scene lock that lasts 2 minutes and is renewed while the editor is open). Saving needs the lock and refuses if someone saved in between.
- Numbering follows Final Draft's workflow, with scene numbers locked instead of pages: **no numbers before the lock** (lists show a dashed draft position); the owner's **Lock script** numbers every scene 1, 2, 3 in order and freezes them (one database step, `lock_script`). After the lock a scene between two others takes a letter (2A, 2B; between 2A and 2B it is 2AA), one added at the end takes the next whole number, one before the first takes A1. **Unlock keeps numbers**; scenes added while unlocked stay unnumbered until the next lock. Decimal numbering (2.1) was considered and rejected because Final Draft, Movie Magic and imports all use letters. Known edge: a scene inserted between 1 and an existing 1A gets 1AA, which sorts after 1A though it sits before it. He said to leave it.
- Revisions per scene (Final Draft colour order: White original, Blue, Pink, Yellow, Green, Goldenrod, Buff, Salmon, Cherry): after the lock, saving changed text tags the scene with the revision in progress and marks the changed lines (coloured bar and an asterisk). The owner or an admin starts the next revision. The database sets the marks, never the browser. Nothing is marked before the lock.
- The Script page looks like one white page in Courier (he does not like blocks): scene heading in bold capitals with a light line under it and a right-aligned Edit button, the scene number in the left margin, a small right-aligned "Write a scene after N" button under each scene. Scene headings stay flush left. There is no title page.
- Import (owner only): Final Draft (.fdx), Fountain, plain text, or pasted text, read in the browser. A review screen lists scenes with flags, lets the owner fix headings, merge, split, leave scenes out, then confirm. A file with scene numbers can arrive locked with its numbers kept (omitted scenes marked); a file without numbers arrives as an unnumbered draft. PDF (text PDFs, read by line position) and Word (.docx, by paragraph styles or indents, else plain text) import are built in `lib/importDocs.ts`; scanned PDFs and old .doc are refused with a message. Both go through the same review screen.
- Matching a new draft to scenes already in the project: by number first, then heading, then alike lines (flagged "Check this match"); line-by-line change view; the owner can change any match; unmatched old scenes are marked omitted after the lock (they keep their number) or removed before it, or kept; a scene someone is editing blocks the draft; applied in one step (`apply_draft`); scene ids and numbers never change; changed scenes get the revision tag.
- Export: PDF in screenplay layout (US Letter, Courier 12, 54 lines a page, scene numbers in both margins, revision asterisks, header with project and revision), Final Draft (.fdx), Fountain. Made in the browser from the scenes the person may read.

**Shot list (first step built)**
- Shots belong to a scene and an owning team. The number (42-1) is computed from the scene number and the shot's place, never stored. Fields: description, size, type, lens, movement, action, cast needed, crew needed, VFX required, sound effects required, special effects, silent, notes.
- The owning team's editors change everything except status. The VFX team and the Sound team can fill their own field on a shot they do not own. Only AD-team members with an edit grant, and owner and admins, change status (planned, done, dropped). Enforced by triggers.
- Drag reordering (grip handle plus the up and down buttons) and the field-by-field merge dialog (when someone saved first, only fields both people changed are asked about) are built. Size, type, lens and movement are `Choice` dropdowns. Not built: script passage link with stale badge, linking cast and crew to the real cast list (text fields for now).

**Storyboard (first step built)**
- One frame per shot (`storyboard_frames`), up to 4 photo layers (each with show tick, opacity, order) plus a pen drawing (strokes as small JSON), edited in a dialog on the Storyboard page. Photos are compressed in the browser (max 1280px, JPEG, about 450 KB or less; input up to 25 MB) and stored in a private Supabase Storage bucket `storyboard` at `<project id>/<shot id>/<file>.jpg`, read through signed links (one hour).
- Mark done flattens layers and drawing into one picture (`flat_path`) and deletes the layer files, which frees space; Reopen turns the flat picture back into layer 1.
- Who edits a frame: the shot's owning team's editors (same rule as the shot). Everyone with view access to Shots sees frames. Enforced by RLS on the table and on storage.objects.
- Per-project size cap `projects.image_cap_mb` (default 200, working value because the brief left it open; owner and admin change it on the Storyboard page; the database refuses growth past it). Deleting a project, scene or shot in the app deletes its files. Known leftover: scenes removed by "apply draft" (unlocked) leave their pictures behind.
- Export storyboard PDF (landscape Letter, 6 frames a page). Offline shows blank frames with a banner.
- Eraser (rubs out pen strokes only) and Move photo, Size and Turn per photo layer are built (stored as optional x, y, scale, rot on each layer in the JSON). Not built: pen pressure, frames in the call sheet.

**Later rules already decided** (in the planning document): call sheets are owned by the AD team and edited and published by AD edit holders, owner and admin; before sending, an authorised person can add or remove recipient emails; a published call sheet gets numbered revisions with changed lines marked; nothing downstream changes automatically when a draft or revision arrives; a call sheet should need a locked script.

## 4. Design system decisions (live in `app/src/index.css`, written up in the planning document part 2)

- Tokens for light and dark, `lb-` class prefix, sans for the interface and Courier only for script views.
- Small corners: radius 2px, 4px, 6px (he found the first 12px too round). No fully round pills.
- Landscape and desktop (1000px and up): the section list is a full-height gray column (`side-gray`: ink 11% over the page background), flush left, open or collapsed to a 64px icon rail. The toggle button has no background, sits at the left of the header row in the column (beside the team and role label), and also answers Ctrl or Cmd + B. The choice is remembered on the device. Each section has its own icon; open shows icon plus name.
- Portrait tablet and phone (under 1000px): no side list and no Sections button. All sections sit in a swipeable strip along the bottom on a light gray bar with white buttons, the current one filled black and scrolled into view.
- New logo from `docs/lineboard-brand/` (light and dark swapped with `<picture>`, mark alone under 360px; favicon, apple touch icon and PWA icons wired in). The old wordmark with a red full stop is gone.
- Shot status is a shape and a word. Dropped rows are struck through.
